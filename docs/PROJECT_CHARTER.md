# Project charter

## Problem and objective
Historical Kyiv alert patterns may change as threats and reporting practices evolve. Investigate whether statistically meaningful temporal patterns exist, remain stable across historical periods, and have predictive value. Full-history training may underperform recent-regime evaluation; this is a hypothesis, not a finding.

## Scope and audience
The geographic focus is Kyiv. Intended audiences are the project owner, data analysts, and research reviewers. Use official intervals and supplementary Telegram message evidence for retrospective research. Exclude operational warning systems, individual-attack prediction, safe-hour recommendations, web applications, database servers, and infrastructure. Task 001 covers documentation and package skeletons only.

## Deliverables and success criteria
Deliver a reproducible validated historical dataset; separate alert, message, and link datasets; transparent multi-label contextual markers; analysis of frequency, duration, structural changes, and regime stability; explicit reporting of absent or unstable patterns; chronological forecast evaluation against simple baselines when justified; and a final report with limitations, uncertainty, and reproducibility instructions. An aggregate research MVP is conditional on evidence.

Finding no stable predictive signal satisfies the research objective if supported by rigorous evaluation. Failure conditions include unverifiable provenance, unresolved data-quality errors affecting conclusions, leakage, unsupported predictive claims, or incomplete reporting of uncertainty. No MVP is required when evidence is insufficient.

## Deadline and governance
Deadline: October 12, 2026, 23:59 Europe/Kyiv. Research and methodology decisions are coordinated outside Codex implementation; Codex implements scoped tasks and records evidence. Provisional targets and taxonomy require review. Prioritize a credible research report over optional forecasting scope.
